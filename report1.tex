\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:Introduction}

\guide{Introduce the project so that the report is self-contained: what the
system or product is, its background and context, and the problem it
addresses. Write directly, and group related sentences into full paragraphs
rather than one sentence per paragraph. Cite your sources, with their dates,
e.g.\ \autocite{lamport_time_1978}.}

\paragraph{Existing Solutions}
\guide{Survey the closest existing systems or related work and cite them:
how each works, and why it is still insufficient for the problem.}

\paragraph{Motivation}
\guide{Explain why the project is worth doing: the gap or need it responds
to, and who benefits from its outcome. Keep this about the problem, not
about applying what you have learnt, which is true of any project.}

\section{Objectives and Scope}
\label{sec:Objectives}

\paragraph{Objectives}
\guide{State the overall objective in one or two sentences of prose (``We
aim to build \ldots\ such that \ldots''). An objective says what the project
achieves; it is not a list of deliverables or deadlines, and the success
criteria belong with the user stories below.}

\paragraph{Scope}
\guide{In a short paragraph of prose, say what the project covers and where
its limits are, with any assumptions and constraints (e.g.\ platform,
language, data sources). State each point once: what is out of scope need
not be repeated as an assumption.}

% Give every user story a unique identifier and conditions of satisfaction
% (CoS) that can be tested. Keep the key stories here; the full backlog goes
% in an appendix.
\paragraph{Requirements}\label{sec:Requirements}
\guide{Give the key requirements as user stories, each with testable
conditions of satisfaction (CoS). The full backlog goes in an appendix.}
\begin{description}[nosep, leftmargin=3em, labelwidth=2.5em, labelsep=0.5em]
    \item[US1] \guide{As a $\langle$role$\rangle$, I want
        $\langle$capability$\rangle$, so that $\langle$benefit$\rangle$.
        CoS: $\langle$a condition that can be tested$\rangle$;
        $\langle$another$\rangle$.}
    \item[US2] \guide{As a $\langle$role$\rangle$, I want \ldots, so that
        \ldots\ CoS: \ldots}
\end{description}

\section{Approach}
\label{sec:Approach}

\guide{Describe how you will carry out the work: the development process
(e.g.\ iterations and what each one delivers) and how progress is tracked.
State what you will use, not what is ``being considered''.}

\paragraph{Architecture}
\guide{Present the planned structure of the system: its components, what
each is responsible for and how they interact. A diagram helps. The reader
should come away knowing how the code will be organised.}

\paragraph{Design Decisions}
\guide{Record the key decisions about the system. For each one, give the
choice made, the alternatives considered and the reason for the choice.}
